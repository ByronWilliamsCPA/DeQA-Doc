## Abstract

The VQualA 2025 Document Image Quality Assessment (DIQA) Challenge, held at ICCV 2025, produced the first large-scale competitive benchmark for perceptual document quality evaluation. Seven verified teams submitted solutions against the DIQA-5000 dataset, which provides multidimensional Mean Opinion Scores (overall quality, sharpness, color fidelity) for 5,000 enhanced document images derived from 500 mobile-captured originals. This paper presents a comprehensive competition analysis covering all seven submissions, six organizer-evaluated baselines, and the dataset design itself. The top four teams all deployed Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs), achieving MainScores of 0.924--0.929 and outperforming the best conventional NR-IQA baseline (RichIQA, 0.866) by 6.7--7.3%. The championship solution (DeQA-Doc, 0.929) introduced pseudo-variance injection and linear interpolation soft labels to overcome the dataset's missing annotation variance. A purely visual CNN approach (GoldenChef, 0.898) and a latent generative method (2077Agent, 0.828) trailed the MLLM cluster, confirming that document quality assessment benefits from cross-modal visual-linguistic reasoning. We identify four cross-cutting technical themes --- MLLM dominance, the missing variance problem, native resolution handling, and test-time augmentation --- and discuss their implications for future DIQA research and the subsequent papers in this technical report series.

## 1. Introduction

The digitization of physical documents through consumer mobile cameras has become the dominant pathway for integrating paper artifacts into digital workflows across legal, medical, academic, and governmental sectors. Unlike natural image quality, which is primarily aesthetic, document image quality is defined by structural integrity, textual legibility, and functional utility for downstream tasks such as OCR. A photograph tolerating minor motion blur may retain its aesthetic appeal; the same blur applied to a document can render it unreadable by text extraction systems.

Document Image Quality Assessment (DIQA) has consequently emerged as a specialized subfield requiring models that understand the semantic relationship between visual degradation and text legibility. Historical approaches relied on surrogate metrics such as OCR character error rate or full-reference methods (PSNR, SSIM) that demand a pristine ground-truth image --- a luxury unavailable in real-world mobile capture scenarios. The VQualA 2025 DIQA Challenge sought to advance the field beyond these limitations by providing a standardized, large-scale benchmark with human perceptual annotations.

**Contributions.** This paper makes the following contributions:

- A comprehensive survey of all seven verified submissions and six baseline models, cataloging architectures, training strategies, and reported MainScores.
- Identification of four cross-cutting technical themes (MLLM dominance, missing variance problem, native resolution handling, test-time augmentation) that explain the performance hierarchy.
- A structured competition analysis that contextualizes the DeQA-Doc technical report series (Papers 1--7) within the broader landscape of DIQA research.

**Series context.** This paper serves as the foundational reference for the DeQA-Doc Technical Report Series. Papers 1--7 drill into specific technical contributions --- VLM benchmarking (Paper 1), cross-domain transfer (Paper 2), prompt engineering (Paper 3), OOD detection (Paper 4), NR-IQA baselines (Paper 5), OCR-IQA correlation (Paper 6), and pseudo-labeling (Paper 7) --- each of which builds on the competition landscape established here.

## 2. Task Definition & Related Work

### 2.1 The DIQA-5000 Dataset

The DIQA-5000 dataset was developed by Shanghai Jiao Tong University and INTSIG Information Co. Ltd as the exclusive benchmark for the VQualA 2025 DIQA track. It contains 5,000 enhanced document images generated by applying six enhancement algorithms (geometric dewarping, demoireing, occlusion removal, deblurring, deshadowing, and appearance enhancement) in randomized combinations to 500 original documents captured with consumer mobile phones.

The source corpus spans diverse content types: dense text-heavy academic pages, complex tabular financial data, scientific diagrams, handwritten administrative notes, and mixed-layout forms. Linguistic diversity includes English prose, Chinese characters, and mathematical symbology. Five primary distortion topologies simulate real-world capture conditions: shadows and uneven illumination, physical occlusions, defocus and motion blur, creases and geometric warping, and moire patterns.

Each of the 500 original documents was processed into 10 enhanced variants, yielding the final 5,000-image corpus. The dataset was annotated by 15 trained human raters using pairwise comparison methodology in controlled viewing environments, following ITU-R BT.500 recommendations for outlier rejection.

Annotations provide continuous Mean Opinion Scores across three orthogonal dimensions:

1. **Overall quality** --- holistic assessment of document utility, readability, and visual clarity.
2. **Sharpness** --- evaluation of high-frequency textual edges, structural definition, and suppression of blurring artifacts.
3. **Color fidelity** --- perceptual accuracy of background chromaticity and foreground ink representations, penalizing color shifts from aggressive deshadowing.

| Partition | Images | Percentage | Source Documents | Annotations |
|-----------|--------|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Training  | 3,500  | 70%       | 350             | Full MOS (3 dimensions) |
| Validation| 500    | 10%       | 50              | Full MOS (3 dimensions) |
| Testing   | 1,000  | 20%       | 100             | Blinded (images only) |

A critical constraint: all 10 enhanced versions of each original document were assigned to the same partition, preventing algorithms from memorizing document structure across splits.

**The missing variance problem.** Unlike established IQA benchmarks (KonIQ-10k, SPAQ, KADID-10k), the DIQA-5000 dataset omits the standard deviation of human annotations. In contemporary MLLM-based IQA, continuous scores are modeled as Gaussian distributions N(mu, sigma^2) to construct soft-label probability distributions over discrete quality levels for cross-entropy training. The absence of ground-truth variance data forced participants to engineer novel workarounds, which became a defining challenge of the competition (see Section 5.2).

### 2.2 Evaluation Protocol (MainScore, SRCC, PLCC)

The VQualA 2025 organizers evaluated submissions using two complementary correlation metrics:

- **PLCC (Pearson Linear Correlation Coefficient)** --- measures prediction accuracy in a linear space, computed after applying a third-order polynomial nonlinear regression to align score distributions.
- **SRCC (Spearman Rank-Order Correlation Coefficient)** --- measures monotonic consistency of rank ordering, independent of absolute score values.

For each dimension, an intermediate DimScore was computed as the arithmetic mean of PLCC and SRCC:

> DimScore_d = (SRCC_d + PLCC_d) / 2

The final leaderboard placement was determined by a composite MainScore that weights overall quality at 50% and the two subsidiary dimensions at 25% each:

> MainScore = 0.5 * DimScore_overall + 0.25 * DimScore_sharpness + 0.25 * DimScore_color

## 3. Competition Overview

### 3.1 VQualA Workshop at ICCV 2025

The VQualA (Visual Quality Assessment Competition) Workshop at ICCV 2025 hosted seven parallel challenges targeting distinct visual quality domains: ISRGC-Q (super-resolution), FIQA (face quality), EVQA-SnapUGC (video engagement), Visual Quality Comparison for LMMs, GenAI-Bench AIGC Video Quality Assessment (Tracks I and II), and the DIQA challenge.

The DIQA track operated on CodaLab with a structured timeline:

- **Development phase**: May 24, 2025 --- release of training and validation data.
- **Testing phase**: July 1--4, 2025 --- blinded test predictions submitted.
- **Report submission**: July 11, 2025 --- technique reports and executable source code required for final ranking.

The competition attracted 120 registered participants organized into 16 teams. During development, teams submitted 183 preliminary predictions to the validation server. In the final testing phase, 16 teams submitted 97 prediction arrays. After the mandatory code reproducibility and fact sheet verification, 7 teams qualified for the official leaderboard.

### 3.2 Baseline Performance

The organizers evaluated six state-of-the-art NR-IQA models on the blinded test set to establish competitive baselines:

| Baseline Model | Architecture | MainScore |
|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| DBCNN         | CNN         | 0.587     |
| HyperIQA      | Self-Adaptive Network | 0.844 |
| StairIQA      | Hierarchical Feature Fusion | 0.850 |
| MUSIQ         | Multi-Scale Transformer | 0.859 |
| TReS          | Transformer Ranking | 0.863 |
| RichIQA       | Advanced Feature Extraction | 0.866 |

DBCNN's MainScore of 0.587 reflects the near-complete inability of static convolutional filters to evaluate document legibility. The transformer-based models (MUSIQ, TReS, RichIQA) performed substantially better but all remained below 0.90, indicating that generalized NR-IQA architectures cannot differentiate between aesthetically pleasing natural textures and structurally vital character edges. Paper 5 in this series presents a detailed analysis of off-the-shelf NR-IQA model performance on DIQA-5000.

## 4. Team Solutions

### 4.1 DeQA-Doc (1st, 0.929) --- mPLUG-Owl2 + Qwen2.5-VL Ensemble

Team DeQA-Doc (led by Junjie Gao) secured the championship with a MainScore of 0.929, representing a 7.3% improvement over the best baseline (RichIQA, 0.866). The team adapted the DeQA-Score framework --- an MLLM originally designed for generic image quality scoring --- to the document domain.

**Pseudo-variance injection.** To overcome the missing variance problem, the team synthesized a dynamic pseudo standard deviation for every training sample. Drawing on empirical analysis of established IQA datasets (KonIQ, SPAQ, KADID), they determined that the average standard deviation of human scoring typically accounts for approximately 20% of the full scoring range. Using the formula sigma_pseudo = 0.2 * range, they normalized all scores to a 1--5 scale and distributed the continuous targets across five discrete quality levels {bad, poor, fair, good, excellent} via Gaussian CDF binning. Paper 7 in this series analyzes pseudo-labeling strategies in depth.

**Linear interpolation mapping.** As a complementary strategy for cases where human variance is assumed to approach zero, the team implemented a binary soft-label scheme. Given a normalized mean score, the algorithm locates the two adjacent discrete level centers and assigns probability mass proportionally, with all other classes receiving zero probability.

**Model ensemble.** The team fine-tuned two foundation MLLMs --- mPLUG-Owl2-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B --- training separate specialists for each of the three assessment dimensions. A "DeQA-Mix" aggregator combined outputs to produce the final predictions.

### 4.2 mapleyzzzz (2nd, 0.927) --- Multi-MLLM Ensemble

Team mapleyzzzz (led by Michael Ao) achieved a MainScore of 0.927 through a massive multi-model ensemble synthesizing three fine-tuned MLLMs: Qwen2.5-VL-7B, mPLUG-Owl2-LLaMA2-7B, and Qwen2.5-VL-32B.

The approach explicitly modeled quality predictions using Gaussian distributions, driving convergence through KL divergence loss. The team employed cross-modal prompt engineering, providing reference points by concatenating original and enhanced images within the prompt: "The original image is image1, the processed image is image2. Rate its quality." The mPLUG-Owl2 models used pairwise comparison constraints with a fidelity loss function. Final predictions averaged the softmax probability distributions across all three models, neutralizing individual architectural biases.

### 4.3 QA-Veteran (3rd, 0.925) --- SigLIP2-NaFlex

Team QA-Veteran (led by Weixia Zhang) achieved a MainScore of 0.925 by building on the SigLIP2 vision-language model with the NaFlex (Native Resolution and Aspect Ratio Flexibility) mechanism.

The core hypothesis was that standard preprocessing pipelines (aggressive downsampling, square cropping) irrevocably destroy the precise optical phenomena --- text edge sharpness, subtle algorithmic blurring --- that DIQA attempts to measure. NaFlex permitted the model to ingest document images at variable native resolutions while preserving original aspect ratios, ensuring localized artifacts and minute defocus blurs were retained in the feature space.

At inference, QA-Veteran used a dynamic textual template: "a photo with {d} {c} quality", where d iterated through assessment dimensions and c represented rating levels 1--5. The model computed cosine similarity between the high-resolution visual embedding and textual embeddings of all template variations, applying softmax to derive continuous predictions.

### 4.4 NJUST-KMG (4th, 0.924) --- MLLM + LoRA + TTA

Team NJUST-KMG (led by Zhe Zhang) achieved a MainScore of 0.924 through an ensemble of three MLLMs: MiMo-VL-7B-RL, Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and Qwen2-VL-7B-Instruct.

**Efficient fine-tuning.** The team froze visual encoder backbones entirely and applied Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) exclusively to text decoder layers, using rank 16 and alpha 32 to introduce only ~7 million trainable parameters per model. Training used the ms-swift framework with AdamW optimizer (lr = 1e-4) over 5 epochs in bfloat16 precision. Aggressive data augmentation (randomized brightness, nonlinear grid distortions, randomized cropping) mitigated overfitting on the relatively small DIQA-5000 training set.

**Test-Time Augmentation (TTA).** Each test image was passed through models four times under deterministic transformations: (1) unaltered original, (2) horizontal flip, (3) gamma correction, (4) Gaussian blur. Predictions were averaged across variants for robustness, then fused across models using a weighted ensemble: MiMo-VL-7B at 40%, Qwen2.5-VL and Qwen2-VL at 30% each.

### 4.5 GoldenChef (5th, 0.898) --- Dual-Pathway CNN

Team GoldenChef (led by Mingying Bai) diverged from the MLLM consensus, achieving a MainScore of 0.898 with a purely visual dual-pathway architecture combining ConvNeXt and Swin Transformer.

The preprocessing pipeline resized images to a larger matrix, partitioned them into 25 non-overlapping sub-image patches, and randomly cropped during training. Two parallel pathways processed these patches: ConvNeXt blocks extracted hierarchical, localized textural features (moire patterns, localized defocus), while shifted-window self-attention mechanisms modeled long-range global context (creases, deshadowing failures). Features from both pathways were concatenated and fed to separate MLP regression heads: one for sharpness and color fidelity, another for overall quality.

### 4.6 2077Agent (6th, 0.828) --- Latent Generative

Team 2077Agent (led by Fan Yang) achieved a MainScore of 0.828 by framing DIQA as a generative prior deviation problem. Their Multi-Expert Feature Fusion Framework with Rectified Flow projected document images into the latent space of a pretrained Stable Diffusion VAE, then extracted features through three parallel experts: a ViT for global attention, a ResNet for localized textures, and a MANIQA module for perceptual quality regression. A Rectified Flow velocity network with multi-head attention served as a structural regularizer, mapping degradation features onto smooth mathematical trajectories toward human MOS values. The lower MainScore suggests that mapping document-specific legibility metrics onto generative natural-image priors introduces excessive semantic noise.

### 4.7 BIT ssvgg (7th) --- Mixture-of-Experts HyperNetwork

Team BIT ssvgg (led by Ruikun Zhang) implemented an Adaptive DIQA Framework with a dynamic Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) system in three stages: semantic extraction via ResNet-50, adaptive perceptual rule learning via a MoE HyperNetwork, and final quality prediction. The hypernetwork routed text-heavy regions to sharpness-specialized experts while directing blank backgrounds to color fidelity experts. No MainScore was reported in the official leaderboard.

## 5. Cross-Cutting Analysis

### 5.1 MLLM Dominance

The four top-ranked teams all employed Multimodal Large Language Models, achieving MainScores in the narrow band 0.924--0.929. This tight clustering --- a spread of only 0.005 across ranks 1--4 --- suggests that the MLLM approach has reached a performance ceiling on DIQA-5000, where marginal gains depend on ensemble strategies and label engineering rather than fundamental architectural innovation.

The MLLM advantage over the best conventional baseline (RichIQA, 0.866) ranges from 6.7% to 7.3%. The gap between the best MLLM (0.929) and the best non-MLLM competitor (GoldenChef, 0.898) is 3.5%, while the gap between GoldenChef and the generative approach (2077Agent, 0.828) is 8.5%. This hierarchy --- MLLM > CNN > Generative --- confirms that document quality assessment fundamentally benefits from visual-linguistic reasoning: models pretrained on large text-image corpora possess superior inductive bias for understanding how physical distortions impact reading comprehension.

The complete failure of DBCNN (0.587) relative to transformer-based baselines underscores that shallow convolutional feature extraction is inadequate for evaluating modern enhanced documents. Paper 1 in this series benchmarks zero-shot VLMs on the same task, providing an additional reference point between pretrained NR-IQA models and fine-tuned MLLMs.

### 5.2 The Missing Variance Problem

The DIQA-5000 dataset's omission of annotation standard deviation forced every MLLM-based team to address the same fundamental challenge: converting continuous MOS values into discrete probability distributions suitable for cross-entropy training.

Three distinct strategies emerged:

1. **Pseudo-variance injection** (DeQA-Doc): Synthesize sigma_pseudo = 0.2 * range based on empirical analysis of other IQA datasets, then apply Gaussian CDF binning over five quality levels.
2. **Linear interpolation** (DeQA-Doc, mapleyzzzz): Assign probability mass to the two nearest quality level centers proportionally to distance, producing binary soft labels.
3. **Explicit Gaussian modeling** (mapleyzzzz): Assume a fixed or learned variance and compute full KL divergence between predicted and target distributions.

The championship solution combined strategies (1) and (2), using pseudo-variance for samples where distributional information is valuable and linear interpolation for high-confidence samples where variance approaches zero. Paper 7 presents a systematic comparison of these pseudo-labeling strategies.

### 5.3 Native Resolution Handling

Document images present a spatial challenge that breaks conventional preprocessing pipelines. A typical mobile document scan exceeds 12 megapixels with critical high-frequency textural data at the character level. Standard deep learning preprocessing (downsampling to 224x224 or 384x384) irrevocably destroys the optical phenomena that DIQA attempts to measure.

Two architectural responses emerged:

- **NaFlex** (QA-Veteran): The SigLIP2 architecture natively processes variable-resolution inputs while preserving original aspect ratios, encoding the full spatial extent of the document.
- **Dynamic patching** (GoldenChef): Partition high-resolution images into non-overlapping sub-patches and process each independently before fusing features, avoiding global downsampling.

Both approaches explicitly reject forced geometric compression. Future DIQA architectures must adopt native resolution strategies as a baseline requirement.

### 5.4 Test-Time Augmentation

NJUST-KMG's TTA strategy --- averaging predictions across four deterministic transformations (original, horizontal flip, gamma correction, Gaussian blur) --- introduced a stabilizing mechanism that proved effective even for relatively strong base models. Because algorithmic document enhancements produce nonlinear artifacts, a single forward pass may hallucinate quality scores influenced by local artifact patterns. Forcing consensus across multiple geometric and radiometric perturbations aligns the model's robust average with the true human perceptual consensus.

Notably, TTA is a test-time-only technique requiring no additional training, making it a low-cost strategy applicable to any existing model. Its effectiveness on DIQA-5000 suggests it should become standard practice for document quality evaluation.

## 6. Discussion

### 6.1 Strategic Insights

The competition results reveal three strategic insights for DIQA research:

**Label engineering matters more than architecture.** The top four teams used broadly similar MLLM architectures (7B--32B parameter scale) but differed primarily in their label construction strategy. The championship margin of 0.002--0.005 over the runners-up was driven by pseudo-variance injection and linear interpolation, not by architectural novelty. This suggests that future DIQA improvements will increasingly depend on data engineering and training signal design.

**Ensembles provide diminishing returns.** Teams 1--4 all employed model ensembles, but the performance spread was narrow. The marginal cost of maintaining multiple 7B+ models in production --- both computational and operational --- raises questions about deployment practicality. A single well-trained model with TTA may approach ensemble performance at a fraction of the cost.

**Document quality is a linguistic concept.** The stark separation between MLLM solutions (0.924+) and non-MLLM approaches (0.898 and below) confirms that evaluating document legibility is inherently a semantic-linguistic task. Models pretrained on text-image pairs possess latent understanding of how distortions affect reading comprehension, an inductive bias unavailable to purely visual architectures.

### 6.2 Implications for Future Work

The competition results have direct implications for several directions explored in this technical report series:

- **VLM zero-shot evaluation** (Paper 1): The gap between fine-tuned MLLMs (0.924+) and organizer baselines (max 0.866) establishes the target range for zero-shot VLM approaches.
- **Cross-domain transfer** (Paper 2): GoldenChef's competitive CNN performance (0.898) suggests that domain-specific visual features remain valuable, even if insufficient alone.
- **OOD detection** (Paper 4): The dataset's limited diversity (500 source documents, mobile capture only) raises questions about generalization to production document populations.
- **NR-IQA baselines** (Paper 5): The baseline results provide the lower bound for any DIQA system, with RichIQA (0.866) representing the best available off-the-shelf performance.
- **Pseudo-labeling** (Paper 7): The missing variance problem defined the competition; systematic study of pseudo-labeling strategies is essential for future DIQA datasets that omit variance data.

### 6.3 Limitations

This analysis is subject to several limitations:

- **Single dataset.** All results are specific to DIQA-5000. Generalization to other document types, languages, or degradation patterns remains untested.
- **Missing per-dimension breakdowns.** The official competition report provides MainScores but limited per-dimension data for individual teams. The per-dimension estimates in Figure 3 are inferred from available reports and should be treated as approximate.
- **No statistical significance testing.** Differences of 0.001--0.005 in MainScore across the top four teams may not be statistically significant at n=1,000, but no bootstrap confidence intervals or significance tests were reported in the competition.
- **Reproducibility.** While teams submitted executable source code, independent verification of all seven solutions was not performed for this analysis.
- **BIT ssvgg.** Team 7 did not report a MainScore, limiting comparative analysis.

## 7. Conclusion

The VQualA 2025 DIQA Challenge established document image quality assessment as a competitive benchmark discipline. The results demonstrate that Multimodal Large Language Models dominate the task, achieving MainScores of 0.924--0.929 that exceed the best conventional NR-IQA baseline by 6.7--7.3%. The championship DeQA-Doc solution (0.929) introduced pseudo-variance injection and linear interpolation soft labels to overcome the dataset's missing annotation variance, establishing label engineering as the primary differentiator among top-tier MLLM approaches.

**Future work.**

- Release of annotation variance in future DIQA dataset versions would eliminate the pseudo-labeling requirement and enable direct comparison of distributional training strategies.
- Native resolution architectures (NaFlex, dynamic patching) should become standard for document quality evaluation, replacing forced downsampling.
- Test-time augmentation provides a cost-free performance boost and should be systematically studied across document types and degradation patterns.
